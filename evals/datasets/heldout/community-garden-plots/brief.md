# Community Garden Plots: Project Brief

Prepared by the Parks and Allotments Team, Ashby Borough Council. Synthetic example; all names are fictional. Date: 14 September 2026.

## Background

Residents apply for one of the 60 plots at Millbrook Community Garden by filling in a paper form at the central library. The waiting list is kept in a notebook in the site office. Applicants rarely hear back after they apply, some wait more than three years, and complaints that plots are given to friends of the committee reach the council every season. Plot holders who stop tending their plot are usually only noticed at the annual inspection in August, by which time the plot is overgrown.

The garden committee, a volunteer group, runs the site day to day. The Parks and Allotments Team owns the land, sets the fees and handles complaints.

## Goals

- Let residents apply for a plot online and see where they are on the waiting list.
- Offer vacant plots to the next person on the waiting list in a fair, visible order.
- Give the Parks and Allotments Team one place to manage plots and plot holders.
- Collect the annual fee without cheques or cash.

## In scope for the first release

- Residents create an account with an email address and a postcode.
- Only residents with a postcode inside the borough can join the waiting list.
- When a plot becomes vacant, the system offers it to the first person on the waiting list by email.
- An offered plot must be accepted within 14 days, after which it is offered to the next person.
- Plot holders pay the annual fee of £45 online by card.
- Staff can mark a plot as vacant, occupied or out of use.

## Out of scope

Selling seeds, tools or compost through the site is not part of this project. The committee's existing seed swap continues on paper.

## Open points

We have not decided how plot holders who neglect their plot should be warned or removed. The committee will discuss this in the spring and send a proposal to the Parks and Allotments Team.
