# Project brief: Fieldwork, job scheduling for field technicians

Prepared by the Operations Change Team at Harlow Water. Synthetic example; all names, places and figures are fictional.

## Background

Harlow Water sends around 140 field technicians a day to repair leaks, replace meters and inspect pipes across three districts. Today the dispatch desk plans the day on a shared spreadsheet and phones each technician with their jobs. When a job runs long or an emergency comes in, the desk rings round to find someone nearby, and customers are often told "between eight and six".

Technicians fill in a paper job sheet on site and hand the sheets in at the depot at the end of the week. Billing and the asset register are updated from those sheets, usually ten days after the work.

## Goals

- Give each technician their day's jobs on a phone, in order, with the address, the customer's access notes and the parts the job needs.
- Let dispatchers see where every job stands and move work between technicians during the day.
- Give customers a two-hour arrival window instead of a full day.
- Capture the job record on site, so billing and the asset register no longer wait for paper.

## Scope for the first release

The first release covers planned work and emergency jobs in the North and Central districts. The South district joins once the first release has run for a full quarter. Technicians will use the company phones they already carry.

## Constraints

- The budget for the first release is 410,000 pounds, including the first year of hosting.
- The first release must be in use by the end of March, before the summer meter replacement programme starts.
- Customer data stays in the United Kingdom.

## Who is involved

The project sponsor is the Head of Field Operations. The Operations Change Team runs the project with the dispatch supervisors, a group of technician representatives and the IT applications team. A supplier, Brightline Systems, builds the app.
